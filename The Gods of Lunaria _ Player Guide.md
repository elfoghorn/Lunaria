# The Gods of Lunaria
*What any character would know | No DM secrets*

---

## The Three Courts — Common Knowledge

The gods are real. Every theological tradition in Lunaria agrees on this, even when they disagree on everything else. The gods do not walk the world. The last time a god's full presence entered the mortal world, the location became permanently wrong — the Coldspire Cathedral is one such scar, a place where the sky itself never healed.

The gods communicate through dreams, omens, blessings given through faith, and very occasionally through miracles large enough that kingdoms notice. Most people in Lunaria have felt the edge of divine attention at least once — a prayer that seemed to be heard, a dream that felt different from ordinary dreaming, a moment of clarity that came from outside themselves.

The scholarly tradition divides the gods into three Courts, each representing a different relationship to the world the First Collision created.

---

## The First Collision — Creation

Before the world, theologians say, there was only the Firstlight and the Firstshadow. Not good and evil — something older than that distinction: the impulse toward form and the impulse toward dissolution. They moved toward each other. From the collision: stars, magic, time, and thirty-six beings produced the way a chord is produced by two notes. The gods.

They shaped the world and then withdrew behind the **Veil Divine** — a barrier that separates divine existence from mortal existence. They cannot enter the world in full presence without tearing it. From behind the Veil they bless, scheme, dream, and watch.

**The Divine Current** is the medium through which divine power reaches mortals. Not direct personal attention from the god with every prayer — rather, a residual creative energy from the First Collision that faith opens like a key. Clerics access it. Gods can strengthen or withdraw their channel. But the Current runs whether or not the god is watching every moment.

## The Celestial Court — Twelve Gods of Growth and Hope

*Worshipped most openly. Shrines in every city. The common person's devotional tradition.*

---

### SOLMAREN — The Dawnfather
**Domains:** Light, Hope
**Holy Symbol:** A golden disc with seven rays, one broken and rejoining
**Sacred Animal:** The dawn-hawk

The most widely worshipped god in Lunaria. Not because he is the most powerful, but because hope is the most universally needed thing. His theology is simple: the morning follows the dark. The effort to make it to morning is the world's most fundamental act.

The Ascendant Church has absorbed him as a subordinate figure called "The Light of Elevation." Most Aethoni don't realise the Dawnfather predates the Calamity by millennia. Outside the Dominion, he is worshipped directly without Church mediation.

**Worshippers:** Healers. Survivors. Anyone who has needed to believe the situation can improve. Most of the general population lights a dawn-candle in his name without formally calling it worship.

**Blessing:** Clarity of mind at dawn. Maximum healing dice on the cleric's first healing spell each day.

---

### VAEL — The Life-Keeper
**Domains:** Life, Nature
**Holy Symbol:** An open hand with a seedling growing from the palm
**Sacred Animal:** The sky-serpent

Goddess of living things continuing — not birth specifically, but continuation. The patron of healers, midwives, and farmers. Her theology holds that living things have a natural tendency to persist that she reinforces. She is not comfortable with what the Below has done to living things that should have died properly and didn't.

**Worshippers:** Healers of every tradition. Agricultural communities. The Verdant Reach's Elder Circle practice is almost entirely Vael-adjacent without using her name.

**Blessing:** Automatic stabilisation of dying creatures within 10 feet of her followers. Clerics restore ability score damage once per day without a spell slot.

---

### THARREN — The Scale-Holder
**Domains:** Justice, Truth (Order in 5e terms)
**Holy Symbol:** Two balanced scales, one pan holding a sword, one a feather
**Sacred Animal:** The iron-hawk

God of consequence that fits the action — not law as written, but earned consequence. What you do comes back, in form and proportion. Institutions can serve this or obstruct it. Tharren's followers are as likely to work against corrupt legal systems as within them.

**Worshippers:** Judges and magistrates (the good ones). Republic Assembly delegates who take their mandate seriously. Investigators of all kinds.

**Blessing:** Cannot be magically compelled to lie. Advantage on saves against deception enchantments. Clerics sense a faint resonance when a formal accusation they witness is true.

---

### LYRETH — The Wordkeeper
**Domains:** Knowledge, Memory
**Holy Symbol:** An open scroll with one line glowing
**Sacred Animal:** The pale raven

Goddess of things being remembered accurately. Not knowledge as power — knowledge as obligation. Every truth known creates a responsibility toward it. The Pale Quill's citation standards are her theology expressed institutionally.

**Worshippers:** Archivists, librarians, historians. The Grand Archive is her most significant institution. The Wall of Names in Freehold is one of her monuments.

**Blessing:** Perfect recall of anything personally witnessed. Cannot misquote from memory. Clerics cast *Speak with Dead* once per day without a spell slot — specifically to gather accurate memory.

---

### OSSIEL — The Full Hand
**Domains:** Harvest (Nature/Life crossover), Community
**Holy Symbol:** A sheaf of sky-wheat with a stone weight attached
**Sacred Animal:** The sky-bison

God of enough — not excess, not scarcity, but the satisfaction of having worked and having what the work produced. He doesn't promise abundance without labour; he promises that honest labour produces enough. In a world where islands can fall, this is not a small promise.

**Worshippers:** The Ashfields farming communities. Republic communal kitchen workers. Anyone who grows, tends, or distributes food.

**Blessing:** In communities that genuinely honour him, crop yields are consistently higher than conditions predict. Clerics purify food and water in community-feeding quantities.

---

### IRENETH — The Shield-Maiden
**Domains:** Protection (War — Defensive)
**Holy Symbol:** A round shield with a visible repaired crack
**Sacred Animal:** The stone-bear (extinct above-ground; her specific Below territory)

Goddess of the choice to stand between harm and what you love. Her symbol says everything: the shield was broken, it was repaired, the crack is visible, this is not a flaw. The crack is the shield's most important feature.

**Worshippers:** Republic Militia. Ironveil Command honour-guard tradition. Parents. Below-entry Runners. Anyone who has put themselves between a threat and someone who couldn't defend themselves.

**Blessing:** Cannot be surprised while adjacent to someone under active protection. Clerics grant temporary hit points to all allies within 30 feet once per day as a bonus action.

---

### AELINDRA — The Beauty-Keeper
**Domains:** Art, Craft (Forge in 5e terms)
**Holy Symbol:** A chisel and paintbrush crossed, bound with sky-silk thread
**Sacred Animal:** The iridescent moth (produces sky-silk; wings are her direct signature)

Goddess of the impulse to make something that wasn't there before. Carpentry, music, mathematics, engineering — all equally hers, because all involve producing something new from what exists. Making is an act of participating in the original creation of the world.

**Worshippers:** Every craftsperson in Stonehollow. Myren's Artisan Tier. The Craftsman VSS class tradition. Verath researchers (intellectual making is making).

**Blessing:** Work produced by her followers is consistently, slightly better than skill alone predicts. Clerics imbue crafted objects with one permanent minor magical property once per month.

---

### CALDREN — The Wayfinder
**Domains:** Travel, Wind
**Holy Symbol:** A compass rose with one arm pointing down instead of north
**Sacred Animal:** The wind-hawk

God of movement between places and the courage to depart. Not adventure — the first step, and the commitment to keep moving. In a world of sky-ocean crossings, departure has genuine spiritual weight.

**Worshippers:** Skyship pilots and crews. Ash-Runners. Kairan long-route merchants. Verdant Reach migration tradition. Pilot Dara Vel wears a Caldren compass-rose on a cord.

**Blessing:** Never lost in clear weather — reliable direction sense when the sky is visible. Clerics cast *Wind Walk* once per day and calm local weather for one hour.

---

### THYREN — The Storm-Caller
**Domains:** Weather, Seasons (Tempest in 5e terms)
**Holy Symbol:** A cloud split by lightning with a green shoot at the base
**Sacred Animal:** The storm-hawk

God of weather as process — necessary, transformative, and not always comfortable. He doesn't promise good weather. He promises honest weather: what the season requires, even when that's destruction. The storm that clears the air. The flood that renews the soil.

**Worshippers:** Skyship navigators. Weather workers. Farming communities that understand seasonal necessity. The Verdant Reach's Wind-Readers.

**Blessing:** Two-hour advance warning of significant weather events. Clerics control weather in a one-mile radius for one hour, twice per day.

---

### MIRETH — The Tide-Keeper
**Domains:** Balance, Commerce (Peace or Order in 5e terms)
**Holy Symbol:** Two hands clasped, neither higher than the other
**Sacred Animal:** The sky-ray (smooth, bilateral, symmetrical)

Goddess of things being even — not equal in the abstract, but equal in the specific transaction. In Kairan commercial culture she is nearly a primary deity: the Nassem Exchange's founding charter invokes her on every page. In the Republic she governs the Assembly vote's meaning: one voice, one vote, regardless of altitude.

**Worshippers:** Kairan merchants. Republic Assembly delegates who believe the vote means something. Judges and mediators.

**Blessing:** Cannot be cheated in commercial transactions — senses structural unfairness. Clerics cast *Zone of Truth* without a spell slot once per day in commercial or legal contexts.

---

### SOLENNE — The Star-Warden
**Domains:** Stars, Guidance (Twilight in 5e terms)
**Holy Symbol:** A ring of seven stars with one larger at centre
**Sacred Animal:** The night-glider

Goddess of finding your way through the dark — not darkness itself, but the navigation of it. Every skyship carries her compass-star engraved on the navigation wheel. The Fringe communities' third-watch prayer, said before ascending to the Watch Tower, is a Solenne invocation.

**Worshippers:** Night-watch crews. Fringe Third Watch workers. Explorers charting unknown routes. Pale Quill archivists who work through the night.

**Blessing:** Natural darkvision to 60 feet. Advantage on Perception in darkness. Clerics cast *Guidance* as a free action in darkness.

---

### BRENNAN — The Hearthkeeper
**Domains:** Community, Fire (Peace in 5e terms)
**Holy Symbol:** A flame above an open door
**Sacred Animal:** The Drift-Hound

God of belonging — the fire at the centre people gather around, the open door, the table where everyone fits. Not hereditary family: chosen community, people who stayed and were welcomed. In the Republic his theology runs so deep it has become the culture's secular value system. The Night of Chains Bread, the Wall of Names, the communal kitchen — all are his, whether or not they use his name.

**Worshippers:** Community leaders and organisers. Republic founding families' descendants. Penitent Way volunteers. Innkeepers who actually care.

**Blessing:** Instinctively trusted by animals and children — not magically, genuinely. Clerics provide long rest benefits from a short rest once per day when leading shared prayer and meal.

## The Equilibrium Court — Twelve Gods of Nature and Balance

*The world's processes rather than its directions. Worshipped by specialists and those who deal with the world's large-scale patterns.*

---

### VASARETH — The Shattered God *(Special)*
**Domains:** Structure, Balance
**Holy Symbol:** A circle with a crack through it

Vasareth is not available as a patron deity — the god is not present to maintain a channel. What this means for the world is a question that has driven theological debate for a hundred years.

The Ascendant Church teaches that Vasareth was elevated during the Calamity and now governs from a higher existence. Other theological traditions have different accounts. What everyone agrees: something significant happened to the god of the world's underlying structure in Year 0, and the world has felt it since.

---

### MORTHUN — The Gate-Keeper
**Domains:** Death, Passage (Grave in 5e terms)
**Holy Symbol:** An open gate in a stone wall, beyond which is light
**Sacred Animal:** The bone-moth

God of what happens when living things stop living — not killing, but the door that opens and the passage through it. His theology is genuinely comforting where honestly understood: death is a door, not an ending. He opens it. He does not control what lies beyond.

The Stonehollow below-commitment tradition (returning the dead to the rock) is his practice. The Penitent Way's funerary tradition. The Wall of Names.

**Worshippers:** Those who work with the dying. Hospice practitioners. Undertakers. The Penitent Way's funerary tradition.

**Blessing:** Creatures under follower care experience no pain in final hours. Clerics see how close to death any creature within 60 feet is. *Gentle Repose* without a spell slot.

---

### KASETH — The Thread-Holder
**Domains:** Fate, Time
**Holy Symbol:** Three threads, two fraying, one uncut
**Sacred Animal:** The grey spider

Goddess of fate's large shapes — not every individual's destiny, but the arcs that must complete. Her theology distinguishes between knowing what will happen and determining it: the thread she holds is a record of decisions made, not a script for decisions not yet taken. Whether this distinction is meaningful is the oldest theological debate about her.

**Worshippers:** Diviners and fortune-tellers. Philosophers. Strategic planners. Those who survived something unpredictable and need to understand why.

**Blessing:** Once per week, a sense of whether a specific course of action will significantly help or harm a primary goal. Clerics cast *Divination* without a spell slot once per day.

---

### OSSIVETH — The Deep Reader
**Domains:** Knowledge, Discovery (Arcana in 5e terms)
**Holy Symbol:** An open eye with a book reflected in the pupil
**Sacred Animal:** The archive-moth

God of the act of finding out — distinct from Lyreth (preserving what is known) and Kaseth (perceiving what will be). Ossiveth is the divine patron of curiosity, the question, the experiment. Knowledge has no moral property in itself. Finding out is neutral and good.

The Grand Archive is his most significant institution. The Verath academic tradition is almost entirely his practice.

**Worshippers:** Grand Archive researchers. Seekers' Concord. Natural philosophers. The entire Verathi academic community.

**Blessing:** Advantage on all Intelligence checks to recall information. Identify magical items by handling for one minute without spell slot. Clerics cast *Legend Lore* once per day.

---

### MORRETH — The Keeper of Secrets
**Domains:** Secrets, Protection
**Holy Symbol:** A sealed envelope with no address
**Sacred Animal:** The shadow-moth

Goddess of secrets that must be kept because revelation would cause harm that keeping prevents. Not deception — that serves the self, which is not her domain. Her theology is about accurately determining whether your secret is self-serving or genuinely necessary. Most secrets fail this test. Some pass.

**Worshippers:** Those who protect others through silence. Intelligence operatives with genuine ethics. Physicians protecting patient confidence.

**Blessing:** Cannot be compelled to reveal information magically — triple-proficiency advantage against disclosure enchantments. Clerics seal documents against all scrying and magical reading.

---

### THYREMOTH — The Stone-Speaker
**Domains:** Earth, Deep Memory (Forge in 5e terms, with geological focus)
**Holy Symbol:** A vertical stone with one horizontal crack through its middle
**Sacred Animal:** The stone-crawler (ancient Below arthropod)

God of the geological record — the information encoded in stone, the memory rock carries, compressed time in the earth's layers. The Stonehollow Compact's entire culture is his theology expressed in daily practice. The Long Memory tradition, the below-commitment funerary practice, the specific reverence for the Ancestor Wall.

**Worshippers:** Stonehollow Compact (the most Thyremoth-devoted kingdom). Geologists and surveyors.

**Blessing:** Sense the basic geological properties of any stone touched — age, composition, recent disturbance, what lies beneath. Clerics communicate with stone as with a slow-speaking creature.

---

### VEYTHREN — The Tide-Turner
**Domains:** Change, Endings
**Holy Symbol:** A wave half-cresting, half-falling
**Sacred Animal:** The turn-hawk (migratory; same bird, different sky each season)

Goddess of things becoming other things — not death (Morthun's domain) but transformation. Her theology holds that fighting transformation is denial, and that the right response to change is understanding what is ending well enough to carry it forward. The Ashkin Republic's founding (Year 0 colony becoming Year 60 Republic) is the most significant Veythren event in recent history.

**Worshippers:** Communities that have rebuilt after disaster. Those who have survived significant personal transformation.

**Blessing:** Adaptation to new environments and circumstances halved in time. Clerics cast *Polymorph* once per day.

---

### SOLANTHIS — The Sky-Walker
**Domains:** Sky, Expanse
**Holy Symbol:** An empty circle — nothing inside
**Sacred Animal:** The Windwhale

God of the sky-ocean itself — the vast expanse of open air between islands. His theology is spare: the sky exists, it is vast, crossing it requires acknowledging its indifference. Do not mistake the sky for safe. He has almost no temples, because temples are enclosed and Solanthis is specifically the god of the unenclosed.

**Worshippers:** Elder dragons (the draconic tradition acknowledges him as the oldest sky-deity). Verdant Reach Dragon-Bond practitioners. Those who have been genuinely alone in open sky.

**Blessing:** No fear of heights; no altitude sickness at any Lunarian elevation. Clerics cast *Fly* at will in outdoor conditions with open sky above.

---

### RENDETH — The Cycle-Keeper
**Domains:** Time, Cycles
**Holy Symbol:** A wheel with seven spokes, each a different season
**Sacred Animal:** The return-dove

Goddess of large cycles — seasons, years, ages, patterns that repeat at different scales. Nothing genuinely ends; everything cycles, the form changing but the essential pattern returning. The World Calendar System is, at its deepest level, a Rendeth document.

**Worshippers:** Agricultural communities. Calendar-keepers. Those who study historical patterns.

**Blessing:** Accurate prediction of seasonal changes up to three months ahead. Clerics cast *Legend Lore* for historical patterns and analogous situations.

---

### SOLVETH — The Balance-Point
**Domains:** Balance, Peace
**Holy Symbol:** A single vertical line with nothing on either side
**Sacred Animal:** The grey dove

God of equilibrium itself rather than any of the things equilibrium produces. He doesn't produce outcomes; he maintains conditions. His theology is the most politically useful and least emotionally satisfying: everything he advocates leads to stability, and stability is not the same as justice.

**Worshippers:** Diplomats and mediators. Treaty lawyers. Those who work to prevent wars rather than win them.

**Blessing:** Can prevent conflict from escalating to violence for one minute through presence and voice alone — creatures must Wisdom save to initiate aggression during active mediation. Clerics cast *Calm Emotions* at will.

---

### AUREATHIS — The Dream-Walker
**Domains:** Dreams, Prophecy (Twilight in 5e terms)
**Holy Symbol:** A closed eye beneath which is another open eye
**Sacred Animal:** The pale owl

Goddess of the dream-space — where the Veil Divine is thinnest and gods communicate most freely with mortals. Not fate-perception (Kaseth's domain) but the prophetic dream: the vision that means something but requires interpretation, the communication that is genuine but not literal.

**Worshippers:** Prophets and dreamers. Those who pay attention to their dreams. The Verdant Reach Elder Circle's communication system operates in her space.

**Blessing:** Perfect dream recall. One piece of genuinely prophetic dream content per week. Clerics cast *Dream* without a spell slot.

---

### CALVEN — The Wild-Keeper
**Domains:** Wilderness, Animals (Nature in 5e terms)
**Holy Symbol:** A tree growing through the roof of a building
**Sacred Animal:** The Elder Dragon

God of everything outside what mortals have built and named. Not opposed to civilisation — simply the force that persists regardless of it. The Verdant Reach Elder Circle is his most formally devoted community. The Elder dragons carry his blessing more directly than any other creatures in Lunaria.

**Worshippers:** The Verdant Reach (most formally devoted of any kingdom). Below-Runners who spend significant time at depth. Sky-fauna researchers.

**Blessing:** Never considered intrusive by non-hostile wildlife — treated as part of the environment. Clerics communicate with any creature regardless of intelligence.

## The Umbral Court — Twelve Gods of Corruption and Ambition

*Worshipped in private, in cults, or through institutional structures that don't acknowledge their divine alignment. Common people know these gods' names as warnings.*

---

These gods are real. Their theology is coherent. People worship them knowingly and unknowingly. A brief summary for player awareness:

| God | Title | Domain | Common worshippers |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Vexar** | The Throne-Taker | Ambition, Power | Commercial empire-builders without ethical constraints |
| **Zhareth** | The Iron Sovereign | Tyranny, Fear | Those who believe hierarchy is moral fact |
| **Morthis** | The Despair-Bringer | Despair, Stagnation | Those who profit from others having given up |
| **Xaelith** | The Entropy-Weaver | Entropy, Destruction | True nihilists; no practical mortal institutions |
| **Vorrax** | The Plague-Bearer | Disease, Corruption | Fragment Fever cults (rare, Below-adjacent) |
| **Sorreth** | The Wound-Keeper | Suffering, Pain | Those who believe suffering grants insight or authority |
| **Vorrnak** | The War-Bringer | War (Offensive), Conquest | Military commanders who prioritise victory over cost |
| **Kessavar** | The Skin-Changer | Deception, Illusion | Political propagandists; sustained institutional untruths |
| **Nethris** | The Hunger | Greed, Consumption | Commercial acquisition without ceiling |
| **Vorrathis** | The Chain-God | Imprisonment, Binding | Those who maintain control through removing options |
| **Vaelthis** | The Wound-Maker | Cruelty, Shadow | Those who cause harm deliberately and with satisfaction |
| **Thorneth** | The Broken Crown | Ruination, Hubris | No willing worshippers; encountered at the moment of catastrophic failure |

**What common people know about the Umbral Court:** They exist. They are real. You do not invoke them. You do not make offerings. If a cleric offers you healing and their holy symbol is one of the above, you should ask careful questions about the terms.

**What common people do not know:** Which institutions are aligned with specific Umbral gods, which current events are products of divine Umbral schemes, or that some Umbral gods have been involved in the world's most significant political events of the past century.

## Religious Practice by Kingdom

| Kingdom | Primary practice | How it looks |
|---|---|---|
| **Aethon** | Ascendant Church (Solmaren absorbed, Tharren adjacent) | Weekly parish attendance; Church present in daily admin; birth/death/marriage records kept by Church |
| **Verath** | Academic — Ossiveth and Lyreth; formal but intellectualised | Shrine visits for researchers before significant projects; citation as theological act |
| **Kairan** | Commercial — Mireth deeply embedded; Caldren before journeys | Three-course welcome before business (Brennan-adjacent); Mireth invocation before significant transactions |
| **Stonehollow** | Thyremoth and Aelindra; ancestral and craft-focused | Long Memory practice daily; shrine in every workshop; below-commitment for the dead |
| **Myren** | Aelindra primary; Kaseth through Salt Court tradition | Aesthetic practice as devotional; fortune readings at significant social events |
| **Republic** | Brennan and Tharren; civic practice not formal religion | Wall of Names; Night of Chains Bread; dawn-candles without calling them Solmaren |
| **Verdant Reach** | Vael and Calven; Solanthis through dragon tradition | Oral acknowledgment, not shrine-based; presence and attention as practice |

> [!player] What This Means for Your Character
> Your character has a relationship to at least one of these gods — even if it's "I don't think about it much" or "I was raised in the Church and I'm complicated about that." Religion in Lunaria is not obligatory flavour. It is a genuine part of the world's architecture. The gods are real, they have specific agendas, and those agendas are playing out around you whether or not you engage with them.
>
> Your cleric or paladin patron is your business. What any character can say is: I know who Solmaren is, I've lit a dawn-candle, I know you don't invoke the Umbral Court names without reason, and I have an opinion about the Church's theology that is probably shaped by where I grew up.
